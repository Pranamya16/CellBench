CellBench detection ruler
Synthetic data · results from the full sweep (5 seeds, 300 + 300 patients per cohort)

Same model, same patients. Only the data changes.

A blood analyser records every cell, but the lab report keeps averages. We planted a known change in some neutrophils of fake patients, trained the same model on the averages and on every cell, and measured how small a change each could still find.

The ruler

Each point is the mean score over 5 seeds (bars show the spread between seeds). 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. The detection limit is the smallest share of changed neutrophils that scores at least 0.80 and stays there.

Limits we state up front

  • All patients are synthetic. Cell positions were measured from four public body-fluid files and borrowed for a blood-like mix; the mix, noise and change sizes are assumptions.
  • The ruler shows what is detectable, not that any real disease produces these changes. It is not a diagnostic tool, and the score is a detector score on a 50/50 synthetic mix, not a chance of disease.
  • The report summary is a stand-in built from simulated cells, not a real lab report. A 5% SSC drop was not detectable by any model.
  • Our fake cells leave the key area of the cell map nearly empty, which flatters the full-cell CNN; real data may be harder.
  • The two CNNs share the model family and training recipe, not the exact layers (their inputs differ). Differences under 0.05 are within seed-to-seed noise.
